# Pasco County / Tampa Bay interior painting: market price + vetting rules (Sept 2026)

Research date: 2026-09-09. Method: WebSearch + WebFetch only. No vendors contacted.
Job spec priced: vacant single-family, 1,200 sq ft floor, 3 bed / 1 bath / living / kitchen, 8 ft ceilings, one color throughout, semi-gloss, two coats. Paintable area assumed ~3,400 sq ft walls + 1,200 sq ft ceilings (+ roughly 400 linear ft baseboard and 8-10 door faces if trim/doors are in scope).

Every number below is tied to its source URL and the date shown on that page. "Pulled" means the page was fetched and read; "snippet" means only the search-engine summary was readable (page blocked the fetch), so treat those as slightly less certain.

## 1. Bottom line: fair price for this exact job (labor + paint)

| Tier | Price | Per floor sq ft | What you get |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low | $2,200 - $2,700 | $1.85 - $2.25 | Small 2-3 person Pasco/Hernando crew, sprayed, contractor-grade paint (SW ProMar 200 or Behr Premium Plus), one color on walls + ceilings + trim, minimal patching. This is the "vacant rental flip" rate. |
| Typical | $3,000 - $4,000 | $2.50 - $3.35 | Insured small company, property-manager turnover rate, walls + ceilings + trim/doors, nail-hole patching, caulk, two coats, cleanup. |
| High | $5,000 - $6,500 | $4.15 - $5.40 | Branded Tampa retail painting company, premium paint (SW Cashmere/Duration), separate trim color, heavier prep. Fine for an owner-occupied home; overpaying for a rental turn. |

Anything above roughly $6,500 for this scope, or above about $5.50 per floor sq ft, is outside every Tampa-area range found and should be challenged.

Rough build-up behind the "typical" number: paint ~$800 - $1,200 (27-30 gal at $28 - $40/gal) + sundries $100 - $150 + labor 32 - 48 crew-hours at $50 - $65/hr per painter (Highmark's quoted Tampa painter rate) = $1,600 - $3,100 labor. Total $2,500 - $4,450.

Biggest pricing variables, in order:
1. Scope: walls only vs walls + ceilings vs walls + ceilings + trim + doors. Ceilings add roughly $1 - $2 per floor sq ft; trim adds $1.50 - $3 per linear ft or $150 - $400 per room; doors $50 - $100 per face.
2. Who is quoting: small owner-operator crew vs franchise/branded company (roughly 2x spread at identical scope).
3. Prep and wall condition: patching, texture (stucco/knockdown common in Tampa adds $0.50 - $0.75 per sq ft), wallpaper, smoke, water stains, primer needs.
4. Paint grade: builder grade $20 - $35/gal vs mid $40 - $60 vs premium $70 - $100. Paint is only 10 - 25% of the job, so grade moves the total by a few hundred dollars, not thousands.
5. One color vs multiple colors; sprayed vacant house (fast) vs cut-and-roll occupied.
6. Semi-gloss on walls: same paint price, but more prep and risk of "flashing," so some painters add 10 - 20% for the extra sanding/priming. See section 5.

## 5. Semi-gloss on walls: what the trade says

Why landlords spec it: washable and scuff resistant, holds up to cleaning between tenants, resists moisture and mildew in FL humidity, cheap to touch up in kitchens/baths. (FG Painting https://fgpainting.com/blog/best-paint-for-rental-property; PainterNearMe https://www.painternearme.com/diy-vs-pro-painting/best-practices-painting-rental-properties; both 2025-26.)

Downsides painters raise:
- Semi-gloss reflects light and shows every drywall flaw, patch, and roller mark; it "flashes" (uneven sheen) over spackled spots unless patches are primed (Hyde Tools https://hydetools.com/blogs/better-finish-blog/flashing-paint-defects; Nash Painting https://www.nashpainting.com/blog/understanding-paint-flashing-sheen-color-and-ceiling-considerations).
- Landlord forum consensus (Brownstoner thread, ~2017-18, https://www.brownstoner.com/forum/general-discussion/painting-walls-semi-gloss-for-rental/): semi-gloss on walls "will scream 1970's tenement"; use it on trim, doors, baths and kitchens, and eggshell/satin on walls. Touch-ups on semi-gloss walls flash, so every turn becomes a full repaint. Repainting over semi-gloss later needs deglossing or bonding primer (extra cost).
- Cost: the paint itself costs the same as other sheens in the same line (UseCalcPro, Jun 2026). The premium comes from prep. Fixr says semi-gloss and high-gloss "cost the most" among finishes; Angi Tampa claims high-gloss "can double or triple" per-sq-ft cost, which is overstated for semi-gloss. Reasonable expectation: 0 - 20% labor adder for the extra sanding/spot-priming, and some crews will refuse to warranty the finish on textured walls.

Typical products: SW ProMar 200 Semi-Gloss (Zero VOC B31 series) https://www.sherwin-williams.com/homeowners/products/promar-200-zero-voc-interior-latex-semi-gloss-pl-9531238; SW Property Solution Interior Latex (landlord line); Behr Premium Plus Semi-Gloss Enamel https://www.behr.com/consumer/products/interior-paint/premium-plus-interior/premium-plus-interior-semi-gloss-enamel.

Recommendation for the spec sheet Lisa's sister sends out: satin or eggshell on walls, flat on ceilings, semi-gloss on trim/doors/bath/kitchen. Same cost, better-looking product, cheaper touch-ups. If the family insists on semi-gloss walls, at least require primed patches and a spray-and-backroll application.

## 6. Licensing and vetting rules, Pasco County FL

### 6a. State license: not required for painting

- Florida Statutes s. 489.117(4)(a) (2025): a person whose job scope does not correspond to a state contractor category "is not required to register with the board," and the statute lists job scopes local governments may not license, including painting, flooring, cabinetry, interior remodeling, handyman services, plastering, stuccoing and caulking. Pulled: https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/489.117
- HB 735 (2021), Preemption of Local Occupational Licensing: preempted local licensing for those job scopes; existing local licenses for them expired July 1, 2023. Bill page: https://www.flhouse.gov/Sections/Bills/billsdetail.aspx?BillId=71241 ; Institute for Justice summary https://ij.org/press-release/newly-passed-bill-eliminates-patchwork-of-local-occupational-licenses-in-florida/ (2021).
- DBPR's own construction FAQ confirms some work such as painting "may not require a state license." https://www2.myfloridalicense.com/construction-industry/faqs/
- Practical consequence, from a FL painting company (Polson Painting, pulled, https://polsonpainting.com/changes-licensed-painting-contractors/): counties used to verify insurance as part of the local license; since July 2023 "this responsibility now falls entirely on consumers."
- One caveat: a painter who also does drywall replacement, carpentry, or anything structural is stepping into licensed scopes; keep the scope to painting and minor patching.

So: a DBPR license search will come up empty for a legitimate painter. That is not a red flag. The 19 DBPR-verified licenses in the demo bench are for the licensed trades, not painters.

### 6b. Pasco County local registration

- Pasco County Contractor Licensing (pulled, https://www.pascocountyfl.gov/services/building_construction/contractors_licensing.php): registration is required "in order for a licensed contractor to pull building permits, be listed as a subcontractor and obtain inspections in unincorporated Pasco County." Painting is not mentioned and interior painting needs no permit, so this does not apply to a painter. Contact: contractorlicensing@mypasco.net, 727-847-2411 (page) / 727-847-8009 (older listing), 8731 Citizens Dr Suite 230, New Port Richey.
- Pasco Code of Ordinances ch. 18 art. IV s. 18-70 (search snippet only; page timed out: http://pascocounty.elaws.us/code/coor_ch18_artiv_sec18-70) still contains a legacy "painting contractor" competency category with a $10,000 net-worth test. Under HB 735 the county may no longer enforce that for painting after July 1, 2023. Treat any "Pasco competency card" a painter shows as a nice-to-have, not a requirement, and expect most not to have one.

### 6c. Business tax receipts (this IS required)

- Pasco County Tax Collector (pulled, https://pascotaxes.com/taxes/business-tax-receipt-information/business-tax-receipt-information/): "Most businesses operating in Pasco County" need a county Business Tax Receipt; "all cities in Pasco County require that a city Business Tax Receipt be purchased for businesses operating within their boundaries," and some cities require the county BTR first. Contractors "must also register with Contractor Licensing prior to performing any construction work" (this line is aimed at permit-pulling trades). Online: https://pasco.county-taxes.com/tcb/app/btexpress/main/start_page
- City BTR pages (from Pasco EDC, pulled, https://pascoedc.com/doing-business-in-pasco-county): New Port Richey https://www.cityofnewportrichey.org/city-departments/finance/business-tax-receipts/ ; Port Richey https://cityofportrichey.com/development-services/ ; Zephyrhills http://www.ci.zephyrhills.fl.us/165/Business-Tax-Receipt ; Dade City www.dadecityfl.com/departments/city_clerk/index.php ; San Antonio and St. Leo have PDF applications. New Port Richey's BTR requires zoning clearance and a building-safety inspection and states the receipt "does not waive local, state, or federal ... licensing" (https://www.cityofnewportrichey.org/files/assets/city/v/1/development/documents/btr/new-folder/2026-business-welcome-letter.pdf).
- A painter based in Hillsborough/Hernando needs a BTR in their home county; Pasco does not require a second one for jobs in Pasco, but a Pasco-based painter with no Pasco BTR is operating informally.

### 6d. Workers' compensation

- Threshold: Florida CFO Division of Workers' Compensation coverage page (pulled, https://myfloridacfo.com/division/wc/employer/coverage-requirements): construction industry "employers with one or more employees, including the owner of the business who are corporate officers or LLC members, must have workers' compensation coverage." Non-construction is four or more.
- Statute: FS 440.02 (2025, pulled, https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/440.02) defines construction-industry employment as "all private employment in which one or more employees are employed by the same employer." Painting is classified as construction for WC purposes (NCCI class 5474; DWC Rule 69L-6.021 lists the construction class codes).
- Exemptions (pulled, https://myfloridacfo.com/division/wc/employer/exemptions/construction): officers of a corporation or LLC members with at least 10% ownership can file a Notice of Election to be Exempt; no more than three officers/members per company; $50 fee; entity must be active on Sunbiz. Renewal is every two years (PEOPayGo, Jul 2026, https://peopaygo.com/2026/07/20/workers-comp-exemption-in-florida-who-qualifies-how-to-file/). "Upon issuance of an exemption, the officer or member is not considered an employee ... and may not recover workers' compensation benefits" (https://myfloridacfo.com/division/wc/employer/exemptions/).
- Key trap: the exemption covers only the named individual. A two-man crew where the owner is exempt and the helper is a "1099 guy" is out of compliance, and an injured helper can look to the property owner. If more people show up than there are exemption certificates plus a policy, stop the job.
- Proof-of-coverage lookup (pulled): https://dwcdataportal.fldfs.com/proofofcoverage.aspx (search by employer name, FEIN, policy number, or all policies by county). Portal home: https://dwcdataportal.fldfs.com/ . Division home: https://www.myfloridacfo.com/division/wc/home . Exemption certificates can be verified from the same portal.

### 6e. What the landlord should require before paying a painter

1. Certificate of Insurance (COI) for general liability, with the owning entity named as certificate holder AND an additional-insured endorsement (a certificate holder alone has no coverage rights; the endorsement does: LegalClarity https://legalclarity.org/florida-certificate-of-insurance-requirements/ ; Greene & Associates https://www.greeneinsurance.com/blog/florida-contractor-general-liability-requirements). $1M per occurrence is the normal ask. Call the agent on the COI to confirm it is in force.
2. Workers' comp: either a WC policy on the COI or a DWC Certificate of Election to be Exempt for each owner on site, verified at the DWC portal above. Count heads on day one.
3. W-9 before the first payment (needed for the 1099-NEC; keep it on file for every vendor over the reporting threshold).
4. Lien waivers: Florida statutory forms in FS 713.20 (https://www.flhouse.gov/Statutes/2025/0713.20/): conditional waiver with each progress payment, unconditional final waiver and release with the final check. Lien rights cannot be waived in advance. If the job exceeds $5,000 the owner should record a Notice of Commencement under FS 713.13 (Levelset FAQ https://www.levelset.com/notice-of-commencement/florida-notice-of-commencement-faqs/). Most single-house paint jobs will fall under $5,000; a batch of several houses on one contract will not.
5. Sunbiz active-status check for the LLC/corp, and a county or city BTR (section 6c).
6. Written scope: product line and sheen by surface, number of coats, prep included (patching, caulk, priming patches), what is excluded, price per house, and a per-sq-ft or per-room rate schedule so repeat jobs across the portfolio are priced the same way.
7. Payment terms: no more than a small deposit for materials on a job this size; balance on walk-through.

Not legal advice; confirm with the family's Florida attorney or insurance agent before adopting as policy.
